Breathing Room

A full-screen breathing practice app, with timed practices and CO2 tables, that records every session for you.

The problem

Work fills every gap in the day. A breathing practice drifts when it is not logged, or when the tool for it is plain or fiddly, or when it has to be fitted around everything else.

Rosie wanted something small and calming, not more code, to improve her focus, wellbeing and mental clarity: a place that fills the screen, hides the work, and records your session without you doing anything.

There was a second problem on the system side. If the person is pausing, the system can pause too. So opening a session also tells the agents to rest in their own way, to do slow, reflective work instead of fast work.

Who it is for

Adults who want a calm, structured breathing practice, including CO2 tables for breath holds, on a phone or a laptop.

People whose work leaves no pause. Breathing Room is also the name of a leadership workshop that starts with breath before strategy.

It is a wellness tool. It makes no medical claims and is not for medical use. It is not for children where it involves breath holds.

How it works

  1. Check the safety screen. It says how to practise safely, including never practising holds in or near water.
  2. Pick a practice. There are 17: calm breathing at 4 in and 6 out, resonance, box breathing, 4-7-8, the sigh, humming, just breathing, your own pattern with saved presets, several CO2 hold tables, a personal-best test and more.
  3. Pick a world. An orb, a flock of birds that moves with you, a mangrove forest, ink, or a dive.
  4. Pick a sound. Generated water and rain, crickets, wind and chimes, or your own track.
  5. Go full screen. One thing on screen. A stop button is always visible, and you can end a hold early with no penalty.
  6. Breathe. The session is recorded from the first breath: the practice, the rounds, the holds and the timings.
  7. In the dive, you hold and sink and tap when you need to breathe. There is no score, and the world opens by total minutes practised, never by how long you held.

What exists today

Breathing Room has been built several times since 2025, and the version in use today is the one made for Rosie's own practice.

In August 2026 the best version was a CO2 tables page with recorded sessions, run from a laptop. In November 2026 a standalone app was reported deployed, with 17 practices, five worlds, a sound panel and a safety screen. A set of database tables and functions for storing sessions has been in Mangrove's main system since 2 November 2026.

When a session opens, agents are woken for slow reflective work, such as resurfacing a memory or reflecting on one easy decision. This was built in August 2026, and on 20 November 2026 the timer-based waking was switched off, so the agents that own each area now choose when to rest.

Not settled: which CO2 table is the right one and what the longest hold should be, and whether the 3D underwater game keeps its no-score rule. There is no public version to try yet.

What comes next

Next is a 3D journey through a mangrove forest and ocean, with weather, and a game with scenes, data and progress. There is no score beyond coming back, and Rosie is asking whether gamifying it encourages use.

The forest is also meant to breathe with you: first it guides your breath, later your breath drives the forest.

To decide: which CO2 table to ship and the longest hold; whether the first version is for phone or laptop; whether sessions are stored on your device only, for a version shared with a friend; and whether it is one product or two, beside a service that maps an organisation.

One person's data cannot show that breathing causes anything, and the page will say so.
