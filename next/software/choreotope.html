Choreotope

A flock of birds you can steer with a phone, a camera or music, and a tool for writing dance movement down and watching it performed.

In development. The flock instrument and the notation workspace work in the browser. Running everything as one installable app on laptop, iPad and phone is the plan, and working with live dancers is still being tested.

Who it is for

Choreographers, dancers, live-visuals artists and musicians who want a flock that responds to a body or a set, and anyone who wants to see how a movement score connects to the movement itself.

Choreotope is an instrument built around a simulated flock of birds. The flock moves and makes sound in response to people: the tilt of a phone, movement seen by a camera, or music. It grew from the murmuration on Mangrove's front page, and sits beside a notation tool that shows one dance phrase as a performing figure, a written score and the numbers the flock can use.

In detail

  1. Tilt the phone to steer the flock: speed, tightness and direction follow the phone's motion sensors, and a shake sends in a falcon that scatters the flock.

  2. Dance in front of a camera and the flock gathers to your movement or scatters from it. The sound follows the height and amount of movement, and nothing leaves the device.

  3. A movement loop pedal: sharp movements are picked up, placed on a 16-step grid and recorded layer by layer as kick, clap, hat and bass, and the recorded beat drives the flock in turn.

  4. One engine for all of it: a single flock and sound engine with a choice of inputs, one shared list of parameters, and two devices that can be linked so one is the screen and the other the controller.

  5. A notation workspace that shows one dance phrase three ways at once: a performing figure, a Benesh-style score and the numbers behind it. It has its own tests.
