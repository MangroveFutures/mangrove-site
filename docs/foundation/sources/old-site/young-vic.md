Young Vic X MANGROVE

AI and redesigning work
mangrove x Gemini

It has been an insightful process working with the Young Vic team as we begin to explore the intersection of human creativity and emerging AI capabilities. Our engagement is rooted in Mangrove's philosophy: that the most exciting frontier lies not in larger models, but in designing collaborative relationships with AI that foster curiosity, deeper coherence, and Human General Intelligence (HGI). We approach work like this as part of our R&D lab, focusing on identifying opportunities to redesign systems for immediate impact while aligning with the exponential speed of change.

1. Discovery Phase: 1-on-1 Conversations

Our initial work involved introductory conversations with key team members across different departments—including Marketing, HR, the Executive Assistant (EA) role, and Production—to map out the current atmosphere, workflow issues, and aspirations regarding AI.

The organisational Vibe and Current Usage

The general mood around AI is mixed, showing both enthusiasm for efficiency gains and significant caution:

Positive Experimentation: The marketing team is already experimenting with AI tools (like ChatGPT) for tasks such as copywriting variations and quick research. They are keen to leverage AI for pattern recognition, such as using existing data for A/B testing email subject lines to improve audience engagement.

Skepticism and Caution: Several team members expressed valid apprehension. Concerns include:
- Moral Implications and Job Security: Worrying about the replacement of roles, particularly entry-level jobs that historically provided a path into the industry. One role, focused heavily on forecasting and dynamic pricing (70% of the job), was identified as particularly vulnerable to automation.
- Creative Integrity and Critical Thinking: Fear that AI use by applicants is leading to "meaningless" AI-generated cover letters, making it harder to differentiate human creativity. A feeling that pervasive AI might lead to human laziness and a loss of critical thinking.
- Data Security and Policy: A wariness of using public models (like regular ChatGPT) for sensitive HR data or financial information due to uncertainty about how the information is sourced and where it is stored. The Young Vic currently does not have an explicit AI policy but recognises the urgent need to develop one.

Bottlenecks and Opportunities Identified

HR & Recruitment: Highly Manual Processes. Recruitment involves manually downloading application forms, moving files to folders, updating separate spreadsheets for shortlisting, and managing monitoring forms, described as "working in the 1980s". This takes significant time (up to two hours a day when actively hiring). Opportunity: Automate the collation, filing, and transcription of applicant data to free up staff time for high-value tasks like current staff support, training, and policy development.

Executive Support: Calendar Management & Note-taking. The EA role is often reactive, spending huge amounts of time coordinating complex meetings (e.g., seven or more people). Minute-taking for board and leadership meetings is also time-consuming, delaying follow-up actions. Opportunity: Implement automated transcription and templated minute creation for quick turnaround and momentum.

Marketing & Access: Creating accessible content is time-consuming, particularly captioning videos and generating alt text for images. Keeping up with the high demand for video content is a struggle for small teams. Opportunity: Utilize AI to rapidly generate accurate captions and context-aware alt text.

Organisational Flow: Disjointed Systems & Cognitive Friction. Many staff use inconsistent or "hacky" personal systems for organisation (e.g., using email inboxes as to-do lists, written notes, and multiple digital platforms). High levels of technological incompetence across the sector lead to basic tech tasks consuming the time of senior staff. Opportunity: Design custom workflows that support individual cognitive styles.

2. Detailed Discussion: The 90-Minute Session

The group session served as a deeper dive, addressing major concerns (sustainability, training, job roles) and introducing Mangrove's approach to modern workflow design and AI collaboration.

Issues Discussed: Sustainability and Job Security

Job Security and the Future of Work: We discussed that since the acceleration of change is unavoidable, the most effective response for the arts sector is proactive design. Rather than clinging to processes (like administrative work) that can be automated, we must define the value of human roles. The challenge for future leaders is defining new entry points for young people, as many historical administrative entry-level jobs will likely be streamlined by AI.

Sustainability: While AI operations use significant energy and water (primarily for cooling data centers), major providers like Microsoft and Google are either currently or working towards being carbon neutral/negative. We emphasized that the most sustainable approach is using smaller, targeted language models for simple administrative tasks, rather than energy-intensive large models—like hiring Einstein to do photocopying.

Designing Better Systems: Human Augmentation

A key focus of the discussion was shifting the perspective from "AI automation" to "human augmentation" and designing systems that adapt to the individual, rather than vice versa.

The "Chief of Staff" Concept (organising Calendars): The AI can function as an accessible, tireless "chief of staff". For roles like the Executive Assistant, this means automating the most laborious parts of schedule management. For example, the system can be instructed to read multiple emails to find agreed-upon times and instantly create consistent calendar invitations, saving significant coordination time and reducing human error.

Explanation of Notebook LM and Collaborative Workflows: We demonstrated a live example of our conversation-first workflow using Notebook LM (Google's research tool, which has similar functionality to emerging Microsoft Copilot features). This system indexed the transcripts from the Young Vic 1-on-1 conversations and in collaboration with humans, rapidly generated summaries, thematic analyses, and mind maps of the discussions.
