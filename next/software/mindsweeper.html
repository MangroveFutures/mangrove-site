Mindsweeper

Upload your ChatGPT or Claude history and get back a searchable wiki, a memory file that works in any AI, and a map of the ideas you keep returning to.

The problem

Most of us have years of thinking sitting in chat logs, notes and shared drives, and we can no longer get at it. You know you worked something out before, and you cannot find where. The good idea at two in the morning is buried by breakfast. People who struggle with this often blame themselves for being disorganised. Mindsweeper starts from the other end: the tools we were given for keeping things are what fall short, not the people using them.

Who it is for

It was first made for people with ADHD and for anyone who feels overwhelmed by their own notes. It suits heavy users of ChatGPT and Claude who want their history to be usable rather than a long scroll. Later it is meant for founders, researchers and consultants who hold large archives of their own work.

How it works

  1. You upload an export from ChatGPT or Claude, as a JSON file or a zip. Later versions take Drive folders, notes, meeting transcripts and handwritten pages as well.
  2. It splits the material into pieces, reads each one, and indexes it so it can be searched by meaning as well as by words.
  3. It finds the structure: the themes, the topics you keep coming back to, and how they connect.
  4. You get three things back. A searchable wiki of your own thinking. A memory file you can hand to any new AI so it starts out knowing you. A map of the ideas you discuss most.
  5. Every item links to the place it came from, so you can check it and go back to the original. You keep the data. This is not meant to be a subscription you are stuck in.

What exists today

Two early versions work. One is a small web tool that takes a pasted braindump and returns it as one well-organised document with the ideas sorted into sections; the other is a custom GPT built for the same job. The full pipeline for whole exports is not a separate product yet. Its method is the one Mangrove ran over Rosie's own archive of about fifteen million words, and that run is the proving ground: what took eighteen months to work out by hand can be drawn out far faster. The pipeline is being moved piece by piece from Mangrove's own platform, Epoch, into Mindsweeper, so that the product and the platform share one method. Which version ships first, and how it is offered, are not decided.

What comes next

The next step is a first version anyone can use: upload one export, get the wiki, the memory file and the topic map back. After that come Drive folders, meeting transcripts and handwritten notes, and a version for organisations that want their own ways of working mapped. A playful way of drawing out what was never written down, in the spirit of Would You Rather, is planned to sit alongside it.
