Instruments

Three small instruments for looking at a record of thinking.

A loop of loops

A torus is a surface you can circle in two independent ways: round the ring, and round the tube. A habit that returns, a project that comes back each season, a conversation that keeps meeting the same question: each winds round both. The line here goes five times round and three times through, and never meets itself on the surface.

A sheet, rolled up

Data with many dimensions often lies on a much simpler shape, like a sheet rolled into a spiral. Points that look far apart across the roll are near neighbours along the sheet. The picture rolls and unrolls; the bright points are the neighbourhood of one walker along the surface.

A hundred thousand poems and more

In 1961 Raymond Queneau, a founder of the Oulipo, published Cent mille milliards de poèmes: ten sonnets cut into strips so that any line can be swapped for the same line of another sonnet, making 1014 poems. This piece follows his method with six lines and eight versions of each. Press a strip to turn it.
