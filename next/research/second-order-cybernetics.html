Second-order cybernetics

The researcher is inside the system, and changes it by observing it.

Go deeper

From outside to inside

First-order cybernetics studies a system from outside, as a regulator and a thing regulated. Second-order cybernetics, which Heinz von Foerster called the cybernetics of cybernetics, puts the observer inside the picture: the one who watches is part of the system and changes it by watching.

What it asks of Mangrove

The researchers are inside the pair they study. There is no neutral seat. So the method is to work in the open loop, keep the record, and treat each design as a way of finding out.

Variety

From the same tradition comes Ashby's law of requisite variety: a regulator needs as much variety as the thing it regulates. Mangrove reads it as a design claim: minds vary naturally, and tools often fail because they offer too little variety, not because people need fixing.

The question

  1. Do systems with more variety and room for tangents fit high-variety minds better than systems that force one straight line?
