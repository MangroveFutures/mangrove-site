BodyScan AI

A guided walk through your body, toe to head, so you can say where things feel different and take a summary to your appointment.

The problem

In a short appointment, people forget things. Something that bothered them last week, a pattern across several parts of the body, a symptom that seemed too small to mention.

A guided scan makes sure nothing is missed. You go through the body in a fixed order, one region at a time, and what you notice ends up in one place you can read, print and compare with last month.

Who it is for

Anyone getting ready for an appointment: a GP visit, a specialist referral, or a stage in a care pathway.

Clinics that want to offer the scan to patients in the waiting room or before they arrive. It is designed to fit any healthcare setting and any condition, and a multilingual version has been considered.

People living with a long-term condition who want to see how things change over time.

How it works

  1. Start at the feet. The scan begins at the toes and works up to the head.
  2. Describe each region. For each part of the body you say what you notice. The second version breaks the body into 23 regions.
  3. Answer the follow-up questions. They help you say more about what you notice. They do not diagnose and they do not triage.
  4. Get a summary. You receive a short written overview of what you noticed, which you can print for your GP.
  5. Come back and compare. Sessions are dated, so you can repeat the scan and see what has changed.

Your answers stay in your own browser.

What exists today

BodyScan AI has been built twice. A first prototype was made in a no-code builder in February 2025 and shown to a GP who was interested. In July 2026 it was rebuilt as a web app that runs entirely in the browser, with no triage, no diagnosis and a printable summary for your GP. The version of 24 July 2026 added finer regions, repeat sessions over time, and two demos, one for a patient and one for a clinician, using invented patients only.

BodyScan AI is not yet open for people to try. The demos are shown on request.

It has not had a regulatory review. Nothing in it should be read as medical advice.

Examples use the demos with invented patients. One shows a patient going through the scan from toes to head. The other shows what a clinician sees: the summary page, and the comparison between two dated sessions.

A sample summary for an invented patient will sit beside the scan, so a reader can see exactly what would be printed for a GP.

What comes next

First, give BodyScan AI a home of its own, so the demos can be opened to the first people who try it.

Then decide how BodyScan AI relates to HealthStory AI: a standalone product, or the quick way in that leads to the longer story. Rosie has called them siblings.

Other ideas on the list, none decided: an analogue printed version, family use, photos of skin changes, and a use in intake for coaching.

The page does not offer a diagnosis, and we have not taken any outside view on where a scan like this becomes a medical device. That will need doing before any clinic uses it.
