Built for people and for agents

This site is being built in two layers: one for people, which adapts to how each person reads, and one for AI agents, which has no buttons. Both draw on the same writing.

People visit pages; agents increasingly read them for people. We are building for both, on purpose, and testing each layer first with its nearest user.

A website now has two kinds of reader. People visit, browse and follow what interests them. AI agents fetch pages, quote them and summarise them, often for someone who never visits. In a Pew Research Center study of browsing in March 2025, people clicked a search result in 8% of searches that showed an AI summary, against 15% of searches without one.

So we are designing this site to be quoted and summarised as well as visited. Those are different jobs, so the site has two layers built from the same writing.

The human layer adapts to the reader: how deep to go, whether to start from pictures or words, and how the page looks and sounds. The tabs above are the first small example. The agent layer has no buttons: each page has a plain text twin, structured data that says what the page is, and later an endpoint agents can ask directly.

Each layer is tested first with the user closest to hand. For people, that is Rosie Collins, who founded Mangrove. For agents, it is Mangrove's own AI agents, which help decide what they would want to find.

Why two layers

People and agents arrive with different needs. A person wants a page that is pleasant and clear to read, at the depth they have time for. An agent wants text it can lift accurately, with the date, the source and the terms of use attached. One page can try to serve both, but it usually serves each of them badly. We would rather build two layers from one body of writing and keep them in step.

The shift is measurable. In a Pew Research Center study of browsing in March 2025, people clicked a search result in 8% of searches that showed an AI summary, and in 15% of searches without one. A growing share of the people who learn about a piece of work will learn about it through an agent's summary. The writing has to survive being summarised.

This follows the rest of Mangrove's work: build systems that adapt to the person, instead of asking the person to adapt to the system.

The human layer adapts

The same writing is offered at more than one depth: a top line, a short read, a long read, and the source. This page is the first to offer that choice, through the tabs above.

A reader can start from pictures or from words. Motion can be turned off. The accessibility kit is on every page by default, not as an extra: it changes text size, spacing, contrast, the colour theme and the font, reduces motion, and reads the page aloud. Look for the "Display and reading" button.

Every page has to work fully with a screen reader. That means real headings and landmarks, labelled controls, image descriptions that carry the meaning of the picture, and nothing that depends on colour or position alone. We test with a screen reader, and we learn from people who use one every day.

The first person the site is fitted to is Rosie Collins, who founded Mangrove. Her taste sets the first version. The depth, form and access controls are how the site then widens to other people.

The agent layer has no buttons

An agent does not need a menu or a theme switch. It needs clean content and a clear statement of what it may do with it. The agent layer has four parts.

  1. A text twin of every page: the same address ending in .md, written to be quoted. Each twin puts its main claim first, keeps paragraphs short and self-contained, and carries its date and sources in the text.
  2. Structured data: semantic HTML, and schema.org descriptions that say whether a page is an organisation, a person, an article or a paper.
  3. Later, an MCP endpoint (Model Context Protocol, a standard way for agents to ask a service questions) over the public writing, so an agent can ask the site a question instead of crawling it.
  4. A stated licence and terms for agents: who may quote the writing, who may train on it, and on what terms.

We also publish an llms.txt file, a short list of the text twins for agents to start from. It is a courtesy, not a strategy. Ahrefs reported in May 2026 that 97% of llms.txt files are never requested. It costs almost nothing, so we add it, but we do not rely on it.

Agents help design the agent layer

Mangrove's own AI agents are the first agent users of the site. Before we build the agent layer in full, we run a small user study with them. We ask each agent: when you need to know what Mangrove is, what it has written or what it offers, what would you want to find, in what form, and what slows you down today? Their answers become the agent layer's requirements, in the same way that interviews with people become the requirements for the human layer.

How the build is recorded

Each page has a design record that says what was decided and why. This page and its text twin are the public account of the approach. The site is also the working example for an essay in progress, In Defence of Tangential Thinking, which argues for systems that fit the way a mind actually moves.

The order of work: this page and its twin; a text twin for every page, generated from the page; llms.txt and schema.org across the new site; the user study with our agents; the depth control tested with a screen reader on more pages; then the MCP endpoint, the licence page and more depths.
